Vertical motion assumptions. WindBorne reports typical ascent and descent rates of 1–3 m/s; the default 2 m/s uses the midpoint. Aerostar’s published 5 m/s ascent is from one large super-pressure balloon launch, not a general maneuvering specification. Aerostar descent is not published here, so its preset uses an explicit 2 m/s descent assumption. The hourly solver moves toward selected wind layers at the chosen rate and enforces a coarse terrain-clearance floor; it omits balloon-specific thermal, buoyancy, envelope, and payload effects. Forecast data options. GEFS ensemble mode routes 31 NOAA members separately; cell colors show the share of those member routes within 100 km, not calibrated real-world probability. GFS deterministic mode uses one forecast path and reports hit/miss, not ensemble confidence. Open-Meteo ensemble models · NOAA NOMADS GEFS GRIB2
Rate limits and data access. This static app requests weather from Open-Meteo in your browser. Its free non-commercial limits are 10,000 calls/day, 5,000/hour and 600/minute; limits apply per client IP and application. Large ensemble responses can be throttled earlier. GEFS uses one multi-location request and caches the data when you adjust lead time or climb/descent. After a 429, the app retries once using the provider’s wait hint; if still limited, wait a minute or choose GFS deterministic. Deterministic mode returns less data but shares the same IP quotas. NOAA NOMADS serves official GRIB2 subsets, but a browser implementation needs to download and decode fields across members and forecast times. Open-Meteo usage limits · NOMADS filter guidance
